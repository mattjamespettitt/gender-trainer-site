What was changed

Nouns only. Words that Lexique or the frequency list find mostly used as another part of speech (être, pas, mía) are left out.

Clear genders only. Nouns whose gender depends on the person (médecin, estudiante), that can take either gender (mar, color), or that are plural-only are left out of the quiz (they are kept for lookup, in other). Nouns that change gender with their meaning (tour, parte) are kept separate, in mixed. Slang and regional senses (Quebec, Latin America and so on) are ignored when deciding a gender, so mano is feminine even though one slang sense is masculine.

Corrections. The Wiktionary extract for French has the wrong gender for some words (for example calamité, osmose and géranium), so for French Lexique's gender is used wherever it has one, and Wiktionary's only where Lexique has none. A small number of other entries were corrected by hand. A few crude words were removed.

Meanings. The English meanings are the short synonyms from the first senses in Wiktionary, with long definitions left out, so they are a cue for the word, not a full definition, and they follow Wiktionary's order. If you see a wrong gender or an odd meaning, please tell me.

Format

{"nouns": [[word, "m" | "f", "meaning", [terms]], …], "mixed": […], "other": […]}

nouns is in order of frequency, commonest first. meaning is the short English meaning shown in the app (up to three meanings joined by " / "), and terms is every English meaning found for the noun, used by the app's lookup.

mixed lists nouns whose gender depends on the meaning: [word, [["m", "meaning"], ["f", "meaning"]]]. other lists nouns the app looks up but doesn't quiz, because their gender isn't a single fixed one: [word, "person" | "either", "meaning", [terms]], where "person" means masculine or feminine according to the person or the meaning (el/la médico) and "either" means either gender is used (el/la mar).
