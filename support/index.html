Why do some questions say "masculine" and "feminine" instead of le or la?

When the article would give the answer away or mislead, the app asks plainly. In French, le and la both become l' before a vowel or a silent h (l'eau, l'homme), so you can't tell the gender from the article. In Spanish, a feminine noun that starts with a stressed a- takes el in the singular (el agua, but las aguas). The app decides by how the word is spelt, for masculine and feminine nouns alike, so the style of the buttons never gives the answer away.

What are the ending rules?

Some endings mostly give the gender: for example -tion, -té and -ie are feminine in French, and -ment, -age and -eur are masculine; in Spanish -ción, -dad and -ía are feminine, and -or, -aje and -o are masculine. Each rule in the app is right at least 85% of the time in the app's own word lists. After each answer the app tells you which rule applies and whether the noun is one of its exceptions (la peau, la fleur, la mano, el día). French has fewer reliable rules than Spanish: about 40% of French nouns end in one of the listed endings, against about 80% of Spanish nouns.

How does Lookup work?

Type an English word to see the French and Spanish nouns for it, or a French or Spanish noun to see its gender, its English meaning and the equivalent in the other language. You can leave out accents and the article (l'eau, el árbol and "the house" all work). The translations are matched by shared English meanings, so they're a good guide rather than a certainty: a noun can have several translations depending on context, and the app shows the likeliest. Only nouns are included, about 28,000 of them, and Lookup is free.

Some nouns aren't in the quiz

The quiz leaves out nouns whose gender depends on the person (le/la médecin, el/la estudiante), nouns that change gender with their meaning (le tour the trip, la tour the tower), plural-only nouns, words that are mostly used as something other than a noun, and a few crude words, so the app can be used by everyone. Most of these can still be found in Lookup, which shows masculine or feminine for them.

How do I open the next arcade level?

Each language has its own seven levels. Pass a level to open the next: reach level 5 in Level up (you go up a level every 5 right answers) or get 18 of 20 in Sprint.

What's free and what's in the unlock?

The 500 commonest nouns of each language are free, with the ending rules, your progress, and the first three arcade levels. The one-time unlock adds every noun (Top 1,000, Top 3,000 and everything), arcade levels 4 to 7 and the Exceptions drill. There's no subscription.

Restoring your purchase

The unlock belongs to your Apple ID and is shared with your family through Family Sharing, so it works on all your devices. If the app shows as locked after reinstalling or on a new device, go to Settings in the app and tap Restore purchase.
